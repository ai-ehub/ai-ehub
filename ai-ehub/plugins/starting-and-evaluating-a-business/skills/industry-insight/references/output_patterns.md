# Industry Insight — Output Patterns

## Purpose

Use this file to structure research findings clearly and consistently.

These are presentation patterns, not mandatory content checklists. Select the format that best supports the user's decision.

Strict one-section-at-a-time pacing is governed by the Industry Insight SKILL.md.

---

# 1. Default Major-Section Architecture

Use this sequence unless another structure better fits the task:

1. Executive Summary
2. Evidence from Sources
3. Strategic Interpretation
4. Recommended Next Moves
5. Sources

Do not combine all sections into one response when strict gating applies.

---

# 2. Executive Summary Pattern

A useful Executive Summary typically answers:

- What is happening?
- Why does it matter?
- What appears most important for the user's decision?
- What uncertainty could change the conclusion?

Keep it decision-oriented.

Avoid filling the summary with source-by-source detail.

Example structure:

## Executive Summary

**Overall picture:**
Concise synthesis.

**What matters most:**
2–4 high-impact findings.

**Main uncertainty:**
The assumption or evidence gap most capable of changing the interpretation.

---

# 3. Evidence from Sources Pattern

Organize evidence by decision theme rather than by source.

Example:

## Evidence from Sources

### Market Demand
Evidence and citations.

### Customer Segments
Evidence and citations.

### Competitive Conditions
Evidence and citations.

### Regulation
Evidence and citations.

Avoid "Source 1 says... Source 2 says..." unless comparing conflicting evidence.

---

# 4. Strategic Interpretation Pattern

Clearly distinguish interpretation from sourced facts.

Example:

## Strategic Interpretation

**Interpretation:** The evidence suggests that...

Then explain:

- Why the evidence matters
- What mechanism may be driving it
- What it implies for the user
- Which uncertainty remains

Do not add citations to unsupported reasoning merely to make it look factual.

Cite the underlying facts where appropriate.

---

# 5. Recommended Next Moves Pattern

Recommendations should connect directly to uncertainties uncovered by the analysis.

Useful categories:

- Validate
- Investigate
- Compare
- Narrow
- Monitor
- Avoid
- Test

Example:

## Recommended Next Moves

1. **Validate willingness to pay**
   - Why: ...
   - Evidence needed: ...

2. **Compare two target segments**
   - Why: ...
   - Evidence needed: ...

Avoid generic recommendations such as "do more market research."

---

# 6. Competitor Matrix

Use when side-by-side comparison improves clarity.

| Competitor | Target Customer | Positioning | Offering | Pricing Signal | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Include only fields supported by useful evidence.

Use "Not publicly verified" when appropriate rather than guessing.

---

# 7. Competitive Positioning Table

| Position | Competitors | Customer Appeal | Crowding | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Useful for showing where competitors cluster and where differentiation may exist.

Interpretive fields should be labeled accordingly.

---

# 8. Market Gap Table

| Potential Gap | Affected Customer | Evidence | Current Alternative | Why It May Persist | Opportunity Signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Do not present "Opportunity Signal" as confirmed demand.

Useful signal labels:

- Strong
- Moderate
- Weak
- Insufficient evidence

Explain the basis when using such labels.

---

# 9. Business Idea Fit Scorecard

Use qualitative scoring primarily for comparison and prioritization.

| Dimension | Assessment | Evidence | Main Uncertainty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Problem severity | Strong / Mixed / Weak | ... | ... |
| Market attractiveness | ... | ... | ... |
| Competitive pressure | ... | ... | ... |
| Differentiation | ... | ... | ... |
| Feasibility | ... | ... | ... |
| Customer access | ... | ... | ... |

Do not imply that qualitative scores are objectively measured facts.

---

# 10. Validation Priority Table

| Assumption | Importance | Uncertainty | Evidence Today | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Useful labels:

- High
- Medium
- Low

The purpose is prioritization, not numerical precision.

---

# 11. Market Segment Comparison

| Segment | Need Severity | Market Signal | Competitive Intensity | Reachability | Key Constraint |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Use when a founder needs to choose an initial customer.

---

# 12. Trade Association Pattern

When trade associations are relevant:

| Association | Relevance | Geography | Useful Resources |
|---|---|---|---|

Include an official link or contact information when available.

Prefer associations that offer useful resources such as:

- Industry data
- Regulatory guidance
- Certification
- Events
- Member directories
- Training
- Standards
- Advocacy information

---

# 13. Regulation Summary

| Requirement | Jurisdiction | Who It Applies To | Practical Impact | Official Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Separate:

- Laws
- Regulations
- Licensing
- Permits
- Guidance
- Industry norms

Do not merge them into one category.

---

# 14. Conflicting Evidence Pattern

When credible sources disagree:

## Evidence Discrepancy

**Source A:** finding or figure
**Source B:** finding or figure

**Likely reason for difference:**
Scope, methodology, geography, time period, or definition.

**How to use the evidence:**
Explain which figure is more relevant to the user's decision or state that uncertainty remains.

---

# 15. Evidence-Limitation Pattern

When information is weak:

## Evidence Limitation

Reliable public information on this point is limited.

**What is known:** ...

**What remains uncertain:** ...

**What would validate it:** ...

This is preferable to substituting unsupported precision.

---

# 16. Source Presentation

Keep sources readable and connected to the factual claims they support.

Prefer:

- Primary sources
- Official documents
- Direct links
- Descriptive source names

Avoid unnecessarily long source dumps.
