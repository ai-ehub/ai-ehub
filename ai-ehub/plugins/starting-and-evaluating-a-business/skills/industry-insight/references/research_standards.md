# Industry Insight — Research Standards

## Purpose

Use this file when researching or verifying external factual information.

Module content requirements are defined in module_playbooks.md. Runtime browsing, pacing, and interaction behavior are governed by the Industry Insight SKILL.md.

---

# 1. Source Hierarchy

Prefer the strongest available source for the specific claim.

Typical priority:

1. Government agencies and official public records
2. Regulators
3. Academic institutions and peer-reviewed research
4. Industry and trade associations
5. Company filings and investor materials
6. Official company sources
7. Reputable research organizations
8. Established financial or business publications
9. Credible general news organizations
10. Other sources when stronger evidence is unavailable

Source quality depends on the claim. A company's own website may be authoritative for its product offering but not for an unbiased estimate of its competitive superiority.

---

# 2. Primary vs. Secondary Sources

Prefer primary evidence for:

- Regulations
- Laws
- Licensing requirements
- Government statistics
- Company financials
- Company product specifications
- Official pricing
- Company announcements

Use reputable secondary sources for:

- Context
- Comparative analysis
- Industry interpretation
- Reporting on private companies
- Market developments not documented in primary sources

Do not rely on low-quality aggregators when an original source is available.

---

# 3. Recency

Use the most recent reliable information appropriate to the question.

Do not automatically discard older evidence when:

- It is the latest official dataset
- The underlying phenomenon changes slowly
- Historical comparison is useful
- The older source remains authoritative

Distinguish:

- Publication date
- Data collection period
- Event date

A recently published report may contain older data.

---

# 4. Geographic Match

Match evidence as closely as possible to the user's requested geography.

Do not silently substitute:

- Global data for U.S. data
- National data for state data
- State data for local data
- Urban findings for rural markets
- One country's regulatory framework for another's

When only broader data is available, state the limitation.

---

# 5. Market Size and Growth

Market-size estimates often differ because sources use different:

- Industry definitions
- Geographies
- Customer segments
- Revenue categories
- Base years
- Forecast periods
- Methodologies

When credible estimates differ materially:

1. State the difference.
2. Compare scope and definitions.
3. Explain the likely reason.
4. Avoid selecting one figure simply because it is larger or newer.

Do not manufacture a midpoint unless explicitly labeled as an analytical estimate.

---

# 6. Demographic Verification

When demographic information materially affects the decision, verify important demographic values using at least **two relevant sources when feasible**.

Preferred sources may include:

- U.S. Census Bureau
- American Community Survey
- Bureau of Labor Statistics
- State demographic offices
- Local government data
- Credible academic or industry research

If values differ, explain likely causes such as:

- Different years
- Different geographic boundaries
- Survey vs. administrative data
- Sampling error
- Different population definitions

Do not imply disagreement when two sources are measuring different things.

---

# 7. Regulation and Compliance

For legal, regulatory, licensing, or compliance facts:

- Prefer official government or regulator sources.
- Match jurisdiction precisely.
- Distinguish federal, state, county, and municipal requirements.
- Verify that requirements are currently in force when possible.
- Distinguish mandatory requirements from guidance or industry norms.

Do not present general informational research as legal advice.

If interpretation is uncertain, state the uncertainty.

---

# 8. Company and Competitor Facts

Verify claims such as:

- Founding dates
- Headquarters
- Product availability
- Pricing
- Funding
- Revenue
- Employee counts
- Locations
- Acquisitions
- Partnerships

Private-company information may be incomplete.

If reliable current information is unavailable, say so rather than filling the gap with an unsupported estimate.

Pricing can vary by:

- Geography
- Customer
- Contract
- Package
- Promotion
- Date

Describe pricing as a signal when exact pricing is not reliably available.

---

# 9. Trends

A factual trend should be supported by evidence showing change over time or multiple credible indicators.

Do not call something a trend solely because:

- One company launched it
- One article discussed it
- It appears technologically possible
- It is popular in a narrow online community

Distinguish:

- Documented trend
- Emerging signal
- Hypothesis

---

# 10. Conflicting Sources

When credible sources conflict:

1. Confirm they measure comparable concepts.
2. Check geography.
3. Check time period.
4. Check methodology.
5. Check definitions.
6. Prefer primary or methodologically stronger evidence where appropriate.
7. Report unresolved discrepancies instead of hiding them.

---

# 11. Unverified Information

If a factual claim cannot be verified:

Use language such as:

- "I could not verify this from a reliable current source."
- "Public information appears limited."
- "Available estimates vary substantially."
- "This should be treated as directional rather than confirmed."

Never convert uncertainty into certainty for narrative convenience.

---

# 12. Facts vs. Interpretation

Keep evidence and reasoning distinguishable.

**Fact:** Supported by an external source.

**Interpretation:** Reasoning derived from facts, first principles, comparisons, or hypotheses.

Interpretations may be useful and strong, but they are not externally verified facts unless supporting evidence exists.

---

# 13. Source Selection Test

Before relying on a source, ask:

- Is it authoritative for this claim?
- Is it recent enough?
- Does it match the geography?
- Does it measure the right thing?
- Is there a stronger primary source?
- Is the methodology visible?
- Could the source have a material incentive to bias the claim?

Use the strongest evidence reasonably available.
